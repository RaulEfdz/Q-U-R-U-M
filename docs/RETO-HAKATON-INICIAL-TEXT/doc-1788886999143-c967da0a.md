## **Challenge Statement** 

###### **How might we transform observations made during customer visits into structured, reliable, and actionable visibility of the technology installed across our customer base?** 

Every day, field service engineers, sales teams, application specialists, account managers, and other customer-facing colleagues visit healthcare facilities and gain valuable insights about the equipment installed at those locations. 

For example, during a hospital visit, a colleague may observe that the customer has several MR systems, CT systems, ultrasound systems, monitoring solutions, or other equipment from different manufacturers. 

Today, much of this knowledge remains unstructured, distributed, or simply in people's heads. 

The challenge is to create a simple and intelligent way to capture these observations and transform them into a structured Customer Installed Base Intelligence dataset. 

## **The Problem** 

We currently have limited visibility into the complete technology landscape installed at many customer sites. 

Customer-facing employees frequently see and learn information that could help us better understand each customer's environment. However: 

- Information may remain in personal notes, conversations, or memory. 

- Capturing information manually can be time-consuming. 

- Equipment descriptions may be inconsistent. 

- Multiple employees may report the same equipment. 

- Observations may be incomplete or uncertain. 

- It can be difficult to aggregate information across hospitals, cities, countries, or regions. 

- Valuable field knowledge is therefore difficult to transform into actionable insights. 

We want to explore how AI, conversational interfaces, automation, and data visualization could solve this problem. 

## **Your Mission** 

Build a prototype that makes capturing customer installed-base observations as easy as having a conversation. 

Imagine that after visiting a customer, a field colleague could open an app or Microsoft Teams and simply say: 

_“I visited Hospital Alpha today. They have three MR systems, two CT systems and four ultrasound systems. Two of the MR systems appear to be around 8–10 years old.”_ 

The solution should interpret the conversation, identify the relevant information, structure it, and update a customer installed-base repository.

## **The Hackathon Prototype** 

Teams do not need to build a production-ready enterprise system. The objective is to demonstrate the end-toend concept. 

#### **Capture → Understand → Structure → Validate → Store → Visualize → Generate Insight** 

For example: 

1. A user submits an observation through Teams or an app. 

2. AI extracts the customer, location, equipment type, quantity, age, and other relevant information. 

3. The system asks questions when important information is missing. 

4. The observation is checked against existing records. 

5. Structured information is stored in a database. 

6. A dashboard or map is automatically updated. 

7. Users can explore the installed-base landscape by customer or geography.

## **Minimum Viable Prototype** 

At minimum, the hackathon solution should demonstrate: 

- Natural-language capture of a customer observation 

- AI extraction of structured equipment information 

- Storage of the observation in a structured dataset 

- Customer-level installed-base view 

- Basic aggregation or visualization across multiple customers 

**Hackathon data guardrail:** Use a small synthetic dataset to demonstrate the concept. Do not use confidential customer information or real competitive information in the prototype. Use fictional customers, manufacturers, equipment models, and locations where appropriate. 

## **Stretch Goals** 

**Voice capture:** Allow a field employee to dictate observations immediately after a customer visit. 

**Photo-assisted capture:** Explore whether equipment information could be extracted from permitted photographs, labels, or other visual information. 

**Duplicate detection:** Determine whether multiple observations refer to the same piece of equipment. 

**Confidence scoring:** Assign confidence based on how complete, recent, or independently confirmed an observation is. 

**Data freshness:** Highlight customer information that has not been verified recently. 

**AI follow-up questions:** Automatically ask the user for the most valuable missing information. 

**Natural-language analytics:** Allow users to ask questions such as: “Show me customers in Brazil with MR systems estimated to be more than seven years old.” 

**Opportunity identification:** Identify customer sites where the installed technology landscape suggests a potential refresh, upgrade, or engagement opportunity. 

## **Design Considerations** 

**Simplicity:** Capturing information should take seconds, not minutes. 

**Adoption:** Why would a field employee use this after every customer visit? 

**Data Quality:** How can the system distinguish an observation from verified information? 

**Trust:** Users should understand where information came from and when it was last observed. 

**Security & Privacy:** Customer information must be handled appropriately, with access controls and responsible data governance. 

**Scalability:** Consider how the concept could eventually work across thousands of customers and multiple countries.
